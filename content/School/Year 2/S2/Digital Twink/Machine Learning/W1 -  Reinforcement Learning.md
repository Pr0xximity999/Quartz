# Into
Reinforcement learning is a type of [[artificial intelligence]] and a type of [[machine learning]]. Machine learning is an umbrella term for algorithms or models that can improve themselves and become better at a certain task.

# Reinforcement learning
Reinforcement learning is the method of training an AI model by rewarding it when it does the task correct and discouraging unwanted behavior. The agent gets input values from its surroundings or environment and essentially “throws shit to the wall and sees what sticks”. An agent is a term used for the reinforcement learning entity that is learning inside the environment.

The neat thing about reinforcement learning is that it is very similar to how people learn. Trying things out to see what works best and what gives the best outcome.

The list of terms that are important for this type of algorithm are the following 5:
- **Agent**: The learning entity that makes the decisions
	- example: car
- **Environment**: The external system that the agent gets most of its inputs from and where it navigates trough. This is what the agent reacts to
	- example: The road, pedestrians, traffic
- **State (s)**: The representation of the current situation the agent is in
	- example: wind speed, distance to traffic, velocity, traffic lights, position
- **Action (a)**: The choice or decision the agent makes in the environment
	- example: steer, brake, accelerate, change lanes, 
- **Reward**: The feedback that the agent gets for doing its task. Usually an addition or subtraction of points.
	- example
		- +50 for driving to the destination
		- -5 for improper lane change
		- -100 for accidents

# Rewarding the agents
Rewards are *cumulative* and the agent tries to maximize this: where it anticipates getting rewards by trying to recreate proper behavior. AKA, number go up, but prioritize long term rewards over short term reward. So even though an agent will look at the rewards in front of it, the decision is also influenced by long term rewards, just like humans.

# Policy
To maximize future points in the current state, it will decide its actions based on the most future rewards. This strategy of finding actions can be expressed as a probability distribution which is called **policy**. This policy is the thing that changes and influences its actions, it can be seen as *the strategy of the agent*.

# Meaning of return Gt
Gt is the total cumulative reward the agent expects from future rewards starting from state st (not only its immediate reward). Index t represents the current time step and corresponds to the state st of the agent.

# Environments
In reinforcement learning, the environment is usually stochastic (means random, not pre-determined). 
- State transitions are probabilistic
- Rewards may be random
- The policy itself can be stochastic
Therefore, the future evolution after taking an action is uncertain.

Training an agent on a static or random environment both works; the agent will learn to adapt in both situations. Random environments will add uncertainty to future rewards.

# Role of the Discount Factor Y
The discount factor is a number between 0 and 1 and it controls the importance of future rewards. Changing Y changes the importance of immediate rewards and how it diminishes over future rewards.

# Exploration vs Exploitation
Exploring leads to the agents trying different actions and it gradually learns about new rewards and how to utilize found ones. This can lead to learning better solutions but it also has a chance of utilizing a worse strategy by trying something new.

Exploitation means using the best strategy the agent knows from past learning. This will lead to immediate and reliable rewards, but might prevent the finding of newer, better strategies.

Balancing exploration vs exploitation is very important when creating a reinforcement learning model.

## The Epsilon-Greedy Strategy
The E-Greedy strategy is a way to balance exploration vs exploitation.

Epsilon is the exploration rate (between 0 and 1, 0 is full exploration, 1 is full exploitation). In practice, epsilon decreases making the agent more “greedy” over time.
