# Linear Economy model
A linear economy is when a product is created, shipped, consumed, and reached its end-of-life. No reproduction.
When a product is done for, it will be destroyed, incinerated or usually, dumped into a big landfill.

# Global textile problem
Every year, 92 million tonnes of textile waste is produced globally. Over the years production has doubled (from 2000 to 2015), while the duration of clothing (its longevity) has decreased by 36%.

- 11% of plastic waste comes from clothing and textiles (polyester)
- 8% of textiles fibers in 2023 is made from recycled sources
- less than 1% of textiles are recycled into new clothing
- Discarded clothing often ends up in low-income countries.
	- Where lack of waste management infrastructure leads to dumping, burning and severe environmental and social consequences
- Textile waste /end-of-life textile often ends up in landfills or incineration plants.
	- In Finland, non-recycled textile ends up in incineration plants
## In the EU
The EU roughly generated up to **10 billion** Kilograms of textile waste. That’s around **16 kilograms** per person in 2020 and **19 kilograms** in 2022. 
- Of this, **4,4 kilograms** per person were collected separately for reuse and recycling
- **11,6 kilograms** per person ended up in mixed household waste
Of the total textile waste, 82% was post-consumer waste.
- The rest was textile waste generated from manufacturing or unsold textiles

In 2022, the textile consumption per person in the EU required on average:
- 323 M$^2$ of land
- 12 M$^3$ of water
- 523 kg of raw materials
- Causing the equivalent of 355 kg of CO2 emission

Since 2025, the Waste Framework Directive mandates to separate collection systems for used textiles
- In Finland, this has been happening since 2023
	- Still, 100 million kilograms of textile waste are discarded annually

# The Circular Economy
![[Vault-data/Attachments/Textile and Circular Economy - circular economy poste.png]]
The circular economy works as follows:
- Take raw materials
- Produce something that lasts for a longer time (sustainable design)
- Distribute the product
- Consume the product
	- Repair and reuse the product
- Collect the product after use
- Recycle, break down, and reuse the materials to design and produce again
	- Take care of residual waste

Using this process, you can take care of a lot more waste. The aims is to keep materials and products in use for as long as possible, by either:
- reusing
	- re-using the product as is
- Repairing and altering
	- sewing a hole in a shirt, for example
- Recycling
	- Using the raw materials of the product to make a new product

Closing these loops prevents waste generation and reduces the consumption of natural resources (yay for the forests)

## Circular economy and textile
![[Vault-data/Attachments/Textile and Circular Economy - textile loop.png]]
Textiles, after production and consumption, can be repaired, rented, or reused to increase its longevity. Properly collecting and breaking down textiles makes them available to be reproduced again as well.

>[!important]
>Reusing products may reduce its durability and longevity. Strands of textiles may be shortened and cannot be used as properly again.
# The SustainaCycle project
The project aims to reduce waste and save resources by recycling and reusing plastic textile and electronic waste.

## Pilot in Imatra, Finland
This project consists of building easily accessible collection points close to where people live. These containers will be able to be placed anywhere so that there’s 24/7 access to a collection point.
