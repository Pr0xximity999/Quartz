Current business literature conceptualizes responsible consumer behavior through several key pillars:
- **Life-cycle responsibility**: Acquisition, usage and disposal of products and services
- **Triple Bottom Line Alignment**: Evaluating purchases based on their environmental, social and economic impact
- **The “3 Rs”**: In the context of circular economy, behavior is defined by the principes of reduction (limiting consumption, reuse (extending life) and recycling (minimizing waste)
- **Social Agency**: A form of “ecological citizenship” where spending is regarded as a vote used to influence corporate behavior and drive social change

# Nurture
Our upbringing, education, social relations or culture can define how we think and act.

>**Culture**: A learned set of shared interpretations about beliefs, values, norms and social practices which affect behaviors of a relatively large group of people.

Culture is something you can compare to a very big and complex computer. Software generates the actions and reactions of human beings in every part of our life.
- Morality and ethics
- ways of thinking and mindsets
- Judgement and ways of argumentation
- Values and norms
- Routines and “normal behavior”

Whats  considered normal or sustainable consumer behavior may vary from culture to culture.

A lot of people think that adapting is useless because “i alone won’t make a difference”, which is a big cultural thing as well. On the other hand, in places like West Africa, people have the mindset of “I have no choice but being sustainable, because otherwise i will contribute to bad effects”.
# System vs linear thinking
When performing consumer decision, keep in mind how it will affect a system. Will taking water out of this well mean others cannot get it who need it? Will buying this shirt be good for the environment?


# Zombie chicken meat
Some farmers in western European countries produce chickens and use meat for…meat. European consumers favor high value cuts (chicken breasts etc), leading to a surplus of other parts like wings thighs and gizzard. 
These other parts are then exported to West African markets at prices often lower than the cost of local production. These frozen products are often transported in unreliable refrigeration, which causes a lot of it to spoil. And of course transporting the meat all the way to Africa costs a lot of fuel and causes pollution.
