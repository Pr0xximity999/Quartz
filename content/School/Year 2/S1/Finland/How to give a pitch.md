# What?
A presentation has 3 parts usually:
- Tell them what you’re going to tell them (intro)
- Tell them (body)
- Tell them what you’ve told them (recap)

20% of your presentation is usually only remembered.

# Structure
## Signposting
Make sure people know where you are in your presentation or when you are moving on to a next part. 
This is called signposting; clearly announce beginnings and endings of each section by either physically changing (moving around) or using phrases like “Turning to….” or “So much for…”.

## Introduction
This is where the base context is laid and any questions are cleared up. People might not know everything about the topic by purely the title or just don’t know much about the topic.
- “Who is this person?”
- “What is the duration?”
- “What is the presentation about?”
- etc…
### ABCD model
Start your presentation in a way to grab the listener’s **attention**. Tell the audience why they should listen, what are the **benefits**? 
But why should they even listen to the presenter? Are they **credible**? What **direction** is the presentation going in? what will happen? Are there any questions from the audience?

## Messages (body)

>**PEEP**: Point, Explanation, Example, Point

It is very important to be very clear about the point you’re making. Audiences will not pay attention and if you are not signalling your point very well, they might miss it. 
This is why you want to use phrases like “which brings me to my next point…”. 
In this way it is clear what to your audience what your most important points are.

## Conclusion
The conclusion should consist of four different elements:
1. Announcing your conclusion
2. Recapping your messages
3. Ending your presentation 
	- Coming full circle
		- If you started with an anecdote, for example, remind the audience of what you told them and conclude that anecdote
	- Adding a call to action (tell the audience to do something/what to remember)
		- “Like and subscribe!”
4. Ask for questions

# Visual aids
## Slide design
>[!important] Rule of 5
>- No more than five lines
>- No more than five words
>You can use less.
>Your slides should not be the subtitle of your speech. They need to support you and have meaning.

Do not put too many distractions in your slides. Use supporting images or graphs that do not need too much explanation. You can do it, IF you know what you are doing. 
You may point at things to show the audience what you are talking about (very important if you have complex images).

## Communicating with the Audience
>[!important] Slide use
>- Point to your audience
>- Point out details on graphs on your slide (as mentioned before)
>- Change your position physically (go on little walks)
>- Be in control
>General body language use comes off as being relaxed and confident (it is okay to be scared!)
>Show confidence! Speak clearly, openly and enthusiastically (or pretend you are :P)

# Takeaways
- Use the core structure:
	- Introduction: ABCD
	- Messages: PEEP
	- Conclusion: Short recap, coming full circle, call to action
- Make attractive visuals: Rule of 5
- Be confident and enthusiastic

When talking to an international group of people, be mindful of their culture when making a joke. Not every culture may find it funny (in some cultures it’s even seen as unprofessional).
