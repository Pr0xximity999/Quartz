# 1. Lighting
>[!important] Dimmers
>Just buy a dimmer and install it! They’re cheap and really easy to install. Trust me, it WILL improve things massively not needing to have general lighting turned up all the way, all the time. Or get a smart bulb if you’re feeling fancy :3

You can have the best furnished home ever. But bad lighting will still ruin it all.

There are two things you can do to improve your lighting
## Have multiple light sources
Multiple light sources will add depth to your room and will make it more **ambient** and **comfy**. Light sources can be broken up into 3 categories. It’s best to have a good mix of these 3.

Try to use mostly secondary lighting, as that creates a more in depth visual of a room.
### General lighting
Overhead lighting, ceiling lights, even your fan light is all general lighting. 

This is also sometimes called **cleaning lighting** because that’s when they should *only* really be used fully turned up. Otherwise, it will look better if you just keep them dimmed or turned off. Try to use these the least unless needed.

### Task lighting
The name kind of say it, lighting that’s used for certain tasks or jobs. You might call them **functional** lights. 

Task lighting could be one of the following:
- Reading lights
	- lights that stand next to an armchair too!
- Kitchen lights (the one that light up your counter)
- Desk lamps
- Night stand lamps

The thing that’s important about these *functional* lights is that…they’re *functional*.

> “*In spite of the fact that past generations spent years researching light fixtures kind to the eye, we still choose to hang naked bulbs on fabric cables since it is trendy*”
> \- *The Interior design handbook*

Just. keep in mind when putting a light somewhere that it fulfills its purpose or function.

### Accent lighting
These ones don’t really exist for lighting up a space for visibility, but more for just…**ambiance**. A tiny light-up conch or some cute lamp you had since you were a kid. They accentuate the room, give it personality.

Vibes lighting would be a good name for this. It doesn’t really do anything, but it makes the rest work better.

## Not all light is the same (temperature)
To no ones surprise, there is multiple types of light. Look at **color temerature** for example. 

Some guy 150 years ago discovered that a piece of coal looked different the more you heated it up. (funnily enough i talked about this in a [[Games/Minecraft/Modpacks/TerraFirmaGreg/Surviving/Heat and fire#Temperature values|terrafirmagreg heating guide]] i half-assed).

Color temperature is depicted by the Kelvin scale:
![[Vault-data/Attachments/Interior Design The kelvin scale.png|499]]

Lower vales mean warmer, redder light, while higher values are colder, bluer light.

Using warmer light, *usually around 2700K*, can take a room from looking cold and sterile to roomy and warm (vibey, even). 

Candles (which were used when electric lighting wasn’t a thing) emit warm light (around 1500K). Thus, using warmer light make it feel more comfortable and human. 

Fun fact! Cooler light can disturb your sleep cycle because it more resembles sunlight which your monkey brain interprets as “gotta stay awake”. That’s why its useful to set up a *blue light filter* on screens like your phone and computer when it gets darker outside.

You might think that colder light is better because you can see better, but do you really always need to see better (referring back to “[[#General lighting|cleaning lights]]”)? It’s a room, not the inside of a fridge.

If you want to see better, use the [[#Task lighting|task light]] for that! Get a colder desk light, but keep your primary light warmer.

>[!important]
>For warm lights, try to get one with around 2700K Temperature and **90 CRI** if it’s an LED. 
>- CRI stands for *Color Rendering Index* and it depics how good it can mimic natural light.
>- A CRI of 80 is usually considered poor and will look bad.
>- The perfect score a lamp can have is 100 CRI.

# 2. Work in 3 Dimensions
Don’t just fill the bottom of your room. Try to use the walls or even the ceilings. Put things on shelves or put up some curtains. Add a **third dimension** to your room.

Keep these 4 tips in mind when putting up curtains:
1. The curtains should be at least around 15 cm higher than the frame of what you’re covering up.
2. The curtains should extend around 30 cm out on the sides of the frame.
3. The curtains should only barely touch the floor.
4. The curtains should be double the width of the frame.
	- This way, it will still have those ripples whenever they’re closed

This makes it seem like the thing you’re covering up is bigger than that it actually is.
## Hanging up art
Hang up some art on the wall! There’s a ton of ways you can hang up art (kinda like composition in a photograph). There are some easy ones you can use for yourself.

Keep in mind that the part you keep your focus on, or **focal point** should be around 1.4 meters from the floor. This is based on average human length, so if your average guest (or you yourself) is a bit taller, hang it up a bit higher.

If you hang your art behind something like a **couch** or other piece of furniture, the width of the art piece(s) should be no wider than 2/3rds of the width of that furniture.

And most of al, frame it, frame it, frame it!! Don’t just glue or tack it on there, browse the internet for a guide how to frame! A cheap frame will look better than no frame, so don’t worry about underdoing it.

>[!important] A tip for movie posters
>Movie posters have acquired the “guy art” title over the years. A good way to prevent this is by getting it in a **foreign language**. It looks more interesting and cool that way.

# 3. Textures(materials) before colors
If you make everything look the same material, it will still look flat, no matter what color it is.

The general rule of thumb is that you should use as many organics as possible. After that use in-organics and then synthetics.

Organics are:
- Wood
- Wool
- Linnen
- Bamboo
- Cork
- Cotton

Just like how [[#Not all light is the same (temperature)|warm light]] feels more natural, organic materials will feel more natural and human as well. We’ve been using this stuff for forever after all. Organics usually have more muted colors too, so they go better with other things.

In-organics are:
- Stone
- Metal
- Glass

This is also somewhat natural and used throughout history, but we weren’t able to manipulate it as easily until recently. These can feel strong and grounding, but overdoing it makes it feel cold and lifeless.

**Leather** is a bit of an in-between. It technically is organic, but it still looks a little sterile when overdone.

Synthetics are:
- Acrylic
- Plastic
- Polyester
- Vinyl

These are made by humans, and pretty recently too (most were made around world war 2). The nice thing about synthetics is that they can be any shape you want. They can be great to sprinkle through your room, but as with in-organics, don’t overdo it.

A hard color plastic chair, a white laminate bookshelf or clear acrylic furniture might look great if used conservatively.


# 4. Furnish Actions
Don’t just think about how to furnish a room for the sake of looks. Just like with [[#Task lighting|functional lighting]], try to also furnish parts based on **function or action**. Make it look like how you function, instead of just making it look decorated.

Put a lamp where you usually read to make a tiny reading corner, for example. Put stuff where they can be used. DON’T put your tv in some random spot with an awkwardly placed arm mount. Put it on the wall directly where you can look to it flat.

## Command Position
Another way to furnish based on action is to think where the **command position** is. The command position is the place in the room that feels the most secure and grounded. Or in other words, the place in bed where you read a book, the place on the couch where you watch tv, even the place on the table/desk where you work/eat.<br>Facing your back to a door or putting yourself right in from of it will make it feel more awkward (or like everyone can look at you from the outside).

## Flow or traffic
Some other way is to think of the **flow/traffic** of any given space; where do people move? If you place your bed between the door and the closet, you’d have to walk allll the way around your bed to get to it. If you have to walk trough teh middle of a sitting space, you’d interrupt a conversation. Make sure that the flow of a room is not disturbed by the furnishing.

A rug is a great way to section off flow spots. Make sure that at least the front 2 legs are on the rug.

>[!important]
>The “conversation space” of any room shouldn’t be much bigger than a 3 meter diameter (or 1.5 meter radius).


# 5. Practical, Beautiful, or Personal
Everything in a room needs to fall under these categories. Books, plants, art, little trinkets, everything. Don’t just buy some generic nature or animal poster just for the sake of having something. **Make sure it has a use** or at least some story behind it.

Place books on your shelf that you have read/are planning to read. Have chess board only when you play chess (otherwise it’s silly and pretentious). 
get a door mat. they look cool.

It’s *your* house. It can be a fun or cool reflection of you. A museum of your life. An extension, if you will. If you put stuff in your house that means something to you, it will feel personal. Generic stuff will just make it seem like any other room.

And hey, if it’s weird or interesting, it'll be a cool [conversation starter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYVQDc9T3sQ). 

# 6. Clean. It. Up.
A good rule of thumb is that 80% of stuff should be out of sight. Don’t make it look cluttered. Give it a home! 
- Use keyhooks, put stuff in bowls! 
- Organize your cords or even hide them.
- Use those cool racks for your shoes.

Fix things, like a jiggly doorknob or a blind that’s hanging loose. Make your bed or sweep your floor every once in a while.

>[!important]
>Do your dishes. Please.

Just. Make sure it looks clean or at least homey. Making things feel a little “lived in” is ok. But don’t make it look abandoned. A clean home is a clean mind.
