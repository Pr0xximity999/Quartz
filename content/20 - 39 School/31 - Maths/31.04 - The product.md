# The product of two numbers
Multiplying two numbers together is called the **product**.

The product is usually mathematically denoted as an x symbol: $\times$

The two numbers used in a product are called the **factors**.

The left number of the product will be added together the amount of times set by the right number (or the left number *times* the right number).

For example:
- The product of 2 and 6 is: $2 \times 6$ or $2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2$
	- 2 and 6 are the factors
- The product of 1 and 3 is: $1 \times 3$ or $1 + 1 + 1$
	- 1 and 3 are the factors
- The product of 3 and 4 is: $2 \times 6$ or $3 + 3 + 3 + 3$
	- 3 and 4 are the factors


# Products and negatives
As explained at [[#The product of two numbers]], multiplication is just how many times you add a certain number together. ($5 \times 3 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15$).

The same is true for negative factors: ($5 \times -3 = -3 + -3 + -3 + -3 + -3 = -15$) <br>Writing this down for two negative factors ($-5$ and $-3$) is a bit more complex, but a way to do it is like: <br> 
$$
\begin{aligned}
-5 \times 2 = -10 \\
-5 \times 1 = -5 \\
-5 \times 0 = 0 \\
-5 \times -1 = 5 \\
-5 \times -2 = 10 \\
-5 \times -3 = 15
\end{aligned}
$$
Starting with a positive numbers and looking at the pattern moving down is one way. But there's an easier way too. It is kind of like the method in [[#The sum, difference, and negatives]].

1. Take both factors in the equation: $-5$ and $-3$. 
	1. If either number is positive, write down a $+$ in front of it: $5$ -> $+5$
2. remove the numbers: $-$ and $-$ 
3. 
	- $++$ will be **yes yes**, which is the same as saying **yes**  ($+$)
	- $+ -$ will be **yes not**, which is the same as saying **not**   ($-$)
	- $-+$ will be **not yes**, which is the same as saying **not**   ($-$)
	- $--$ will be **not not**, which is the same as saying **yes**   ($+$)
4. The outcome of step 3 will decide whether the result is positive or negative: $-5 \times -3 = 15$

>[!important]- **Keep in mind that the second operator isnt actually an operator**
>It is an indicator that the number is negative. The dual operator trick i showed is just to make adding/subtracting with negative numbers easier.

>[!important]- Tip: If you have a hard time knowing the outcome at all
> Since whether the outcome is positive or negative has been solved, you can remove all minuses, calculate the outcome, and add the minuses back.<br> $-5 \times 3$ -> $5 \times 3 = 15$ -> $-5 \times 3 = -15$

>[!note]- in case the equation has more than 2 numbers
>If for example you have $5 \times -3 \times -4$, you apply the [[20 - 39 School/31 - Maths/31.06 - Order of operations|order of operations]] and do the equation in groups of 2 (in this case left to right).<br>OR, you could count the number of minuses and, if it's an even amount, the solution is positive and vice versa.
